What this document is
This companion document describes the physics, equations, and design decisions behind the SDS-FAR v2 simulator. It supersedes the March 2026 PEARL-GATE Complete Mission Physics document. The simulator is a feasibility exploration framework. Limitations: no CFD, no neutron transport, no plasma instability modelling, no transient structural analysis, no full relativistic particle cascade simulation. All specific values should be read from the simulator — this document explains what those values mean and how they are computed.

1. Architecture: What SDS-FAR Models

SDS-FAR is a Spallation-Driven Subcritical Fragment+Ablator Rocket. It is the simplified 0-D physics representation of the PEARL-GATE Beam-Target Propulsion architecture, tractable for real-time parametric exploration while remaining physically grounded.

The core concept: a spallation beam drives a subcritical fission assembly. Fission fragments are produced at relativistic speeds (~4–8%c). A fraction ε escapes aft as direct thrust. The remainder (1−ε) is trapped and deposits its energy into a graphene ablator, which sublimates and is expelled as a high-velocity jet. A Phononic Crystal Thermoelectric (PCT) array converts waste heat into beam power to close the feedback loop.

2. Governing Equations

2.1 Mass Partition

m_wet = m_dry × R (R = mass ratio) m_prop = m_wet − m_dry (total propellant) m_nuc = m_prop / (1 + K) (nuclear fuel) m_abl = K × m_nuc (graphene ablator) m_jet = (K + 1 − ε) × m_nuc (jet mixture = ablator + trapped frags) m_free = m_prop − m_jet (free fragments = ε × m_nuc)

2.2 Fragment Physics (Relativistic)

γ = 1 / √(1 − v_ff²/c²) (Lorentz factor) KE_unit = (γ − 1) × c² (specific KE of fragment, J/kg) Q_ff = (1 − ε) × KE_unit (energy deposited in ablator per kg fuel) NOTE: v2 uses rapidity-corrected fragment contribution: u_ff = c × atanh(v_ff / c) (rapidity, more rigorous than γv at v > 3%c)

2.3 Ablator Jet

E_ab_mix = η × Q_ff / (K + 1 − ε) (energy per kg of jet mixture) v_p = √(2 × max(0, E_ab_mix − H_sub)) (ablator jet speed, m/s) H_sub = sublimation enthalpy of graphene ≈ 50–60 MJ/kg

2.4 Combined Exhaust Velocity (v2 rapidity-corrected)

v_eff_rapidity = (ε × u_ff + (K + 1 − ε) × v_p) / (1 + K) v_eff = c × tanh(v_eff_rapidity / c) (coordinate exhaust velocity) v1 formula (retained for classical mode): v_eff_std = (ε × γ × v_ff + (K + 1 − ε) × v_p) / (1 + K) At v_ff = 4%c: rapidity correction ≈ +0.07 km/s (small but real — saves 1 day at 550 AU) At v_ff = 8%c: rapidity correction ≈ +1.7 km/s (more significant)

2.5 Mission Δv (Relativistic Tsiolkovsky)

Δv = c × tanh(v_eff / c × ln(m_wet / m_dry)) (relativistic) Δv = v_eff × ln(R) (classical mode)

2.6 Thrust

F_ff = ε × ṁ_nuc × γ × v_ff (fragment thrust, relativistic momentum flux) F_jet = (K + 1 − ε) × ṁ_nuc × v_p (ablator jet thrust) F_tot = F_ff + F_jet ṁ_nuc = P_boot × Π₁ / KE_unit (steady-state mass flow from boot power) ṁ_tot = (1 + K) × ṁ_nuc (total propellant flow) ṁ_jet = (K + 1 − ε) × ṁ_nuc (jet mixture flow) Π₁ = 1 / (1 − k_eff) (subcritical gain = neutron multiplication)

2.7 Subcritical Power Budget

P_nuc = ṁ_nuc × KE_unit P_esc = ε × P_nuc (escaping fragment power → direct thrust) P_trapped = (1 − ε) × P_nuc (trapped → ablator heating) P_abl_in = η × P_trapped (Marshak-coupled to ablator jet) P_unman = (1 − η) × P_trapped (unmanaged waste heat → PCT/radiator)

2.8 Phononic Crystal Thermoelectric (PCT) — v2 correction

v2 derives T_hot from actual Q_rej and explicit areal density (not hardcoded 2 MW reference): rad_area = rad_mass / ρ_areal (ρ_areal = kg/m², user slider) T_hot = (Q_rej / (σ × ε_emit × rad_area))^0.25 (Stefan-Boltzmann, physically derived) carnot = 1 − T_amb / T_hot η_TEG = carnot × (√(1+ZT) − 1) / (√(1+ZT) + T_amb/T_hot) (Ioffe formula) P_PCT = η_TEG × P_unman Q_rej = P_unman − P_PCT

2.9 Self-Sustaining Ratio (Π₃)

P_to_beam = P_PCT × f_beam (fraction of PCT output to beam) Π₃ = P_to_beam / P_boot (must be ≥ 1.0 for self-sustaining operation) SSR ≥ 1.0 → TEGs sustain beam without external power

6. Why the Rapidity Correction Matters

At first glance, the difference between v1 and v2 at 4%c appears negligible — γ = 1.0008, barely above 1. The rapidity correction adds only ~0.07 km/s to v_eff. Yet this propagates through the rocket equation and mission geometry to produce a one-day difference in transit time to 550 AU. One day of consumables on a 4.6-year crewed mission is not negligible.

v1: uses γ·v_ff in Tsiolkovsky numerator (correct for momentum thrust but approximate for Δv) v2: uses rapidity u = c·atanh(v/c) — the rigorous relativistic treatment At v_ff = 4%c: u = c·atanh(0.04) = 12,009 km/s vs γ·v = 12,010 km/s (Δ = 1 m/s) At v_ff = 8%c: u = c·atanh(0.08) = 24,097 km/s vs γ·v = 24,077 km/s (Δ = 20 m/s → +1 day)

The v1 formula is not wrong — it is self-consistent for momentum thrust calculation. The v2 rapidity formula is more rigorous for integrating the Tsiolkovsky equation at relativistic speeds. The simulator implements both and reports the delta explicitly.

8. Beam-Target Propulsion: Category Definition (Unchanged)

BTP is defined by three simultaneous conditions: (1) a high-energy particle beam driver is generated and fired aboard the spacecraft; (2) it strikes a high-Z condensed-phase target inducing hadronic cascade reactions including spallation, fission-fusion and pion/kaon showers; (3) the energetic reaction products are expelled directly as directed thrust.

SDS-FAR models conditions (1) and (2) in simplified 0-D form. The full PEARL-GATE architecture includes condition (3) with hadronic cascade multiplier Π₁ up to 293×, Double Punch wakefield boost (×1.200), and ISM kinetic energy harvesting — none of which are yet in the simulator.

Prior art
No prior propulsion system satisfies all three BTP conditions simultaneously. ADS (Rubbia, MYRRHA) uses conditions (1) and (2) but treats products as shielding and waste, not thrust. ICF pulse (Daedalus) uses onboard beams but fires at low-Z targets — no hadronic cascade. PEARL-GATE is the first and only example of Beam-Target Propulsion.

10. Simulator Limitations

SDS-FAR is a zero-dimensional lumped-parameter model. It resolves energy flows and momentum integrals correctly but cannot predict:

  • Spatial effects: no temperature gradient inside ablator, no beam profile, no plasma density distribution
  • Stability: no plasma instability modelling, no neutron transport, no criticality transient analysis
  • Structures: no transient thermal-structural coupling, no ablator erosion geometry
  • Radiation: no full relativistic particle cascade simulation, no radiation damage accumulation
  • Fluid dynamics: no CFD, no magnetohydrodynamics, no plasma sheath modelling

These require 1-D/2-D MHD, PIC simulation, and neutronics codes. The 0-D model is the necessary foundation — it reveals the amplification chain and the parameter space. It cannot replace higher-fidelity models for engineering design.

What the simulator does correctly
Fragment kinetic energy partition, ablator sublimation thermodynamics, relativistic exhaust velocity (rapidity-corrected), Tsiolkovsky Δv, thrust and acceleration, PCT efficiency (Ioffe formula), radiator thermal balance (Stefan-Boltzmann, v2), three trajectory modes (sprint-only, normal, full-burn coast), mission timing and fuel accounting. These are all conserved-physics calculations with no free parameters beyond the user-set sliders.
