📖 PEARL-GATE 0‑D Simulation Model

Purpose & Scope

This simulator illustrates the relativistic multi‑species thrust concept at the core of the PEARL‑GATE architecture. It is a 0‑D engineering model – it does not resolve spatial or time‑dependent plasma dynamics, but captures the dominant energy and momentum flows using ordinary differential equations (RK4). The numbers are indicative, not flight‑ready.

Unified Thrust Equation (Relativistic Momentum Balance)

Fnet = Σ[ ṁi · γi · vi ] − Fdrag

Where the sum runs over three exhaust species:

  • Fission fragments (FF) – ~4%c, bulk mass (bass)
  • Tamper ablation plasma – ~4%c, thermal ballast (mid‑range)
  • Pion decay products – ~99%c, high‑energy component (treble). Note: pions decay extremely fast; most decay energy (∼70%) escapes as neutrinos. Collimation is assumed ideal – a strong simplification.

Each species is treated as an independent momentum flux. The Lorentz factor γ = 1/√(1‑v²/c²) accounts for relativistic effects in the exhaust. Full relativistic treatment would require the energy‑momentum tensor; this model uses directed momentum flux only.

Energy and Power Flow

Nuclear energy released per pulse (Qtot) is split into:

  • Exhaust kinetic energy – contributes to thrust
  • Thermal energy – heats the reactor core
  • Radiated energy – lost to space
  • Escaping neutrinos/γ – lumped as “ν/γ escape” (a loss channel)

The thermoelectric (TE) veins convert core heat into electricity, which powers the linear induction accelerator (LIA) and the UV laser that initiates the reaction. This closed‑loop power balance is idealised – real conversion efficiencies are lower and degrade over time. Energy conservation is enforced at each RK4 step; the ν/γ escape term closes the balance.

Performance Indicators (Π‑group ratios)

The following dimensionless diagnostic ratios are calculated from simulation variables. They are not derived invariants but useful indicators of how the model behaves.

  • Π₁ = Qtot / Pbeam – Energy multiplication factor
  • Π₂ = Qtot / (ṁc²) – Nuclear conversion efficiency
  • Π₃ = Qout / Qin – Thermal balance ratio
  • Π₄ = PTE / Qtot – Thermoelectric conversion fraction
  • Π₅ = μ = mabl / mcore – Tamper mass ratio (affects ablation thrust)
  • Π₆ = ṁISM / ṁtot – ISM augmentation fraction
  • Π₇ = ṁash·vash / Fthrust – Momentum coupling efficiency (ideally near 1)

Thermal‑Acoustic Coupling (Heuristic)

vexhaust ∝ √( η·Qtot / mash )

This states that the exhaust velocity scales with the square root of energy per unit ejected mass – a standard result from energy conservation. In the simulation, the prefactor is absorbed into the energy partition coefficients. The term “acoustic tuning” is a metaphor for the efficiency with which the beam energy couples to the ablation layer; it is not a derived plasma equation.

End of Life (EOL) & Drag

When the kilopower reactor can no longer sustain the UV laser, the funnel and beam are deactivated. Only passive drag remains:

Fdrag = ½ ρISM vship² Across

The LTD capacitor continues to discharge residual heat as weak data pulses until exhausted. This EOL phase is purely illustrative – actual ISM drag at relativistic speeds would involve more complex plasma interactions. At relativistic velocities, the correct drag force would involve plasma and radiation interactions; the non‑relativistic formula is a placeholder for illustrative purposes.

MHD Braking (Optional Concept)

The simulator includes a placeholder for magnetic braking, but it is disabled in the flyby‑only version. The formula shown is:

Fbrake = ṁISM · (γin vin + γout vout)

This represents a perfectly efficient magnetic sail that reverses the captured ISM momentum. Real efficiency would be much lower (ηMHD ≪ 1) and depends on magnetic field strength.

Limitations & Known Simplifications

  • No spatial or temporal plasma dynamics – 0‑D only
  • Neutrino losses are lumped into a single “escape” term
  • Pion collimation and decay are assumed ideal – not realistic
  • Thermal‑acoustic coupling is a heuristic, not a first‑principles law
  • Π groups are diagnostic ratios, not Buckingham invariants
  • Relativistic efficiency is not optimised – the model favours high exhaust velocity
  • MHD braking efficiency is not modelled (disabled in this version)

This simulator is an educational and speculative tool. It is not intended as a validated design for a real spacecraft, but as a way to explore the relationships between fuel, power, thrust, and relativistic velocity in a multi‑species exhaust system.
