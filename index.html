1 · What is Beam‑Target Propulsion?

Beam‑Target Propulsion (Kazik Huppert, 2024) is the use of products from Nuclear Beam‑Target Physics reactions as propulsive exhaust. A nuclear accelerator (the GATE) fires a particle beam into a solid sub‑critical high‑Z target (the PEARL). The beam‑target collision triggers spallation cascades, fission, fusion, and — at sufficient beam energy — hadronic (pion) production. The rearward-ejected reaction products constitute thrust.

Not to be confused with Beamed Propulsion (Robert Forward), where an external source pushes the craft from behind.

2 · PEARL‑GATE Architecture

GATE = Gridded Accelerating Transit Ejector — the nuclear accelerator (FFAG / Linear Induction Accelerator / MPD).

PEARL = Power Escalatory Autophagic Renewable Locus — the solid compound high‑Z target and booster stage.

Target composition: Natural Uranium + Lithium Deuteride in a Hafnium Carbide matrix, laced with potentially billions of SiGe thermoelectric nanowires. Minimum three 120° elements converging into a closed 360° locus, convex on both the beam‑facing interior and the aft‑facing exterior.

Starter assembly: Kilopower fission reactor (10 kW) + LTD capacitor banks + UV laser + LiH pad, used to ignite the GATE at bootstrap until the three equal tall cylindrical fuel rod wedges are hot enough to power the TE converters self‑sufficiently.

3 · Conservation of Momentum — The Ballistic Pendulum

The GATE is physically coupled to the PEARL. Shooting the target with the onboard beam would produce zero net force if the target were rigid — the gun recoil cancels the target impact. The system gains momentum because the PEARL's aft face spalls and ejects mass rearward. This asymmetric mass loss breaks the cancellation:

Δp_ship = −Δp_exhaust = Σᵢ ṁᵢ γᵢ vᵢ dt

Each kilogram of exhaust ejected at relativistic velocity γᵢvᵢ delivers an equal and opposite impulse to the ship. The ship accelerates toward the shooter, i.e., forward.

4 · The Autophagic Locus Nozzle

There is no permanent nozzle and no electromagnetic confinement. The target itself is the nozzle. High‑tensile winches continually pull the base apexes of three equal tall cylindrical wedges, inclining their aft ends towards each other; the convergence point serves as target, reaction centre, and nozzle. The convergence is self‑consuming (autophagic) and as each layer ablates away is continuously replenished (renewable) thanks to the continual tension of the tethers, until the structural mass reservoir of the three tall sectors has been depleted.

Thermal gradient: Plasma‑temperature reactions occur only at the converged aft end apexes of the fuel rod's three wedges. The segment length and the density of the HfC matrix provide a shadow shield and temperature drop that keeps the winch hooks and GATE hardware well below structural failure temperatures.

Radiation shielding: The full column of dense high‑Z material (U/HfC) between the reaction zone and the ship acts as a shadow shield attenuating gamma and neutron flux by inverse‑square over the segment length.

5 · Multi‑Species Plume

At each beam‑target event the exhaust is a mixture of species, each carrying a fraction of the total nuclear energy release Q_tot:

  • Fission Fragments (FF) — heavy ions, ~4 %c, dominate propellant mass flow
  • Fusion Plasma (D‑T) — lighter ions, ~10 %c
  • Hadronic / Pions (π) — near‑light speed (~99 %c), highest specific impulse, smallest mass flow
  • Tamper Ablation — vaporised HfC matrix carrying waste heat aftward (~4 %c)

6 · 0‑D Governing Equations

Zero‑dimensional: all quantities are volume‑averaged over the control volume; no spatial resolution. Assumes axial symmetry and 1‑D relativistic kinematics.

State variables: m(t) total mass · v(t) velocity · γ(t) = (1−v²/c²)^−½ · E_core(t) internal thermal energy

Mass conservation

dm/dt = −Σᵢ ṁᵢ + ṁ_ISM

Beam‑target gain (Π₁)

Q_tot = Π₁ · P_beam

Π₁ = G_base · (1 + k_fus · f_fus + k_had · h_had)

G_base: baseline fission gain · f_fus, h_had: dimensionless channel fractions · k_fus, k_had: user-supplied multipliers

Species exhaust velocity (relativistic)

γᵢ = 1 + (ηᵢ · Q_tot) / (ṁᵢ c²)  →  vᵢ = c√(1 − γᵢ⁻²)

Energy partition (consistent with Π₁)

q_FF = Q_tot / (1 + k_fus·f_fus + k_had·h_had)

q_fus = q_FF · k_fus · f_fus  |  q_had = q_FF · k_had · h_had

Denominator identical to Π₁ formula — energy budget and momentum budget are algebraically consistent.

Relativistic momentum equation (RK4 integrated)

d(γmv)/dt = F_thrust − F_drag

F_thrust = η_dir · Σᵢ ṁᵢ γᵢ vᵢ

a = F_net / (m γ³)   [longitudinal relativistic acceleration]

Internal thermal energy and TE conversion

dE_core/dt = η_th · Q_tot − P_TE − Q_rad − Q_tamper

P_TE = η_TE · η_th · Q_tot  |  P_beam = η_beam · P_TE + P_aux

Radiative loss (Stefan–Boltzmann)

Q_rad = ε σ A (T⁴ − T_amb⁴)

ISM drag (low‑fidelity, Regime 7)

F_drag = ½ ρ_ISM v² A_drag

Time dilation

dτ = dt / γ   [proper time advances slower than Earth time at v > 0]

Energy closure check

Q_tot = Σᵢ ṁᵢ c²(γᵢ−1) + dE_core/dt + Q_rad + Q_ν

Q_ν = neutrino / lepton escape (unrecovered loss channel, shown as "Lepton Sink" in UI)

7 · Π‑Group Diagnostics

  • Π₁ = Q_tot / P_beam — beam‑target amplification
  • Π₂ = Q_tot / (ṁ_tot c²) — specific energy ratio
  • Π₃ = Q_out / Q_in — thermal recovery fraction
  • Π₄ = P_TE / Q_tot — thermoelectric conversion share
  • Π₅ = ṁ_tamper / ṁ_core — tamper ratio (dimensionless, ≥0)
  • Π₆ = ṁ_ISM / ṁ_tot — ISM scram contribution
  • Π₇ = (Σ ṁᵢ γᵢ vᵢ) / F_thrust ≈ η_dir⁻¹ — directional coupling
  • Π₈ = (Q_rad + Q_tamper) / Q_in — thermal bypass fraction; high Π₈ justifies winch survival

8 · Identified Closure Gaps

  • η_dir(t): pion collimation efficiency — 3‑D hadronic cascade, not resolved in 0‑D
  • Burn fraction f_fus: D‑T ignition probability vs. beam pulse parameters
  • Winch tension dynamics and resonance damping (Atonal Scaling)
  • ISM Coulomb drag at v ≥ 0.1c — magnetic funnel assumed but not modelled
  • Gain multipliers k_fus, k_had: user‑set; physically motivated but not self‑consistently computed
