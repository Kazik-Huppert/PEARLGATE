Each split's second term is formed by subtraction from its parent, so the partition closes by construction.

Relativistic throughout, and the burn runs at constant thrust with constant mass flow, not at constant proper acceleration. Both give the same rapidity w = vₑ·lnR, but under constant thrust the ship is heaviest when it is slowest, so the velocity history is convex and the burn leg covers materially less ground — about 22% less at R = 4. That is why the burn-leg mean speed β̄_burn is integrated rather than assumed.
v̄ = f·β̄_burn(β, vₑ) + (1−f)·β  ·  β = tanh(k⁻¹·vₑ·lnR/c)  ·  F = ṁ·vₑ  ·  κ = ρτ/(2εσT⁴)
A flyby spends atanh(β) once, a rendezvous twice, so at equal burn fraction the rendezvous costs exactly double the rapidity. The two are not otherwise mirror images: the braking leg runs light and quick, so it spends a larger share of its time at speed and β̄_burn is higher for a rendezvous than for a flyby at the same β.
